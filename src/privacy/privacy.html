Privacy policy

ClearView — Content Protection · last updated 15 October 2026

Blocking settings

ClearView is a content-protection extension. It checks what you are about to see on your own device, and it is built so that the ordinary act of browsing sends nothing anywhere. This page says exactly what stays on your device, what can leave it, and how to remove it.

In short
  • Websites and searches you visit are checked on your device and are never uploaded.
  • ClearView collects no browsing history, no account details and no device identifiers.
  • Nothing leaves your browser unless you ask to block a keyword or channel for everyone, or report a mistake. That request carries a random install id and nothing about you.
  • There is no account, no sign-up and no advertising. ClearView has no trackers and no analytics.
What stays on your device
  • Every page address, search query and YouTube title ClearView looks at is evaluated locally, against a rules bundle the extension holds. It is never sent to a server.
  • When a page is blocked, the blocked page is told only a reason code — never the address that was blocked and never the term that matched it — so neither is reprinted on screen or left in your history.
  • The count of items blocked belongs to you alone and stays on the device.
What can leave your device

Only two actions send anything, and both are started by you:

  • A request to block something for everyone. When you ask for a keyword or channel handle to be reviewed for the shared blocklist, ClearView sends the value you typed, the kind of request, and a random install id (below).
  • A false-positive report. When you report that a rule blocked something it should not have, ClearView sends that rule's value and the same install id.

ClearView also fetches the shared blocklist and the status of your own requests. These requests carry the install id and nothing else. The backend never receives your browsing history, the pages you visited, or the searches you made.

The anonymous install id
  • A random id is generated once, when ClearView is installed. It is derived from nothing about you: not from your email, your account, your hardware or your location.
  • It exists so that a contribution is not counted twice and so your own requests can be shown back to you. It is shown, read-only, in the settings page.
  • It is created once and never replaced, so it cannot be refreshed into a new identity on its own.
What is stored, and where
  • Settings — your switches, blocked keywords and websites, and blocked channels — are stored in Chrome's synced storage. If you have turned on Chrome Sync they follow your signed-in Chrome profile, exactly as your other browser settings do.
  • Local data — the cached shared blocklist, the anonymous id, your blocking counts, and the Protection password — is stored on this device only and is never synced.
  • The Protection password is never stored as text. Only a salted PBKDF2-SHA256 hash is kept, on this device, and it is never sent anywhere. It is a device lock, not an account.
  • Removing the extension from Chrome deletes all of this local data. You can also delete your own keywords, blocked websites and channels at any time from the settings page.
Permissions, and why they are asked for
  • storage — to keep your settings, your caches and your password hash.
  • webNavigation — to see the address you are navigating to, so it can be checked and, if it matches a rule, stopped.
  • declarativeNetRequest — to refuse blocked hosts and YouTube ad requests before they load.
  • alarms — to refresh the shared blocklist periodically.
  • contextMenus — to offer the right-click “Block this channel” entry.
  • Host access to the ClearView server — to exchange shared rules and review requests. Access to any other server is requested only if you point the extension at your own.

ClearView does not read the content of pages you visit. Its content script runs only on YouTube, and only to hide Shorts, remove ads and filter the feed.

The ClearView server
  • Requests go to the ClearView backend, whose address is shown in the settings page's Backend server row. Self-hosters can point it elsewhere.
  • The server stores the shared rules and the review queue. It does not store page visits, search queries or browsing history — it is never sent them.
  • Administrators who review submissions sign in to the server directly. Their credentials are handled by the backend and are never held in the extension.
Children
  • ClearView is meant to be used with a parent or guardian, or by an adult. It asks for no personal information from anyone, and it creates no account for anyone.
Changes, and contact
  • If this policy changes, the “last updated” date at the top of this page changes with it.
  • Questions about this policy, or a request to remove something you contributed, can be raised through the project's issue tracker.
